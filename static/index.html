CHAPTER 01 — THE QUESTION

What makes an iris recognizable?

Look at your eye in a mirror. The colored ring is not a flat color. It has fine lines, folds, openings, and tiny shapes.

But photographs change. The camera moves. The light changes. Your pupil grows and shrinks. Before a computer can compare two eyes, it must solve a harder question:

How do we turn two different photographs into patterns measured in the same way?

CHAPTER 06 — RESEARCH & VALIDATION

How far can we trust the result?

HAWIYA is a working educational prototype, not a certified biometric identity system. Explore exactly what is measured, what is inferred, and what remains unverified.

Pipeline provenance

YOLO Iris V2 locates iris and pupil masks; geometry fits circles; the original normalization maps the ring to 64 × 512 samples; ArcIris ResNet100 extracts two 512-D descriptors from grayscale and gamma-adjusted normalized textures.

Live scan results are computed on the Python backend. The interactive strip, histogram, preview exposure and gamma experiment run in your browser. The two results are intentionally labeled separately.

What the confidence score does — and does not — mean

YOLO confidence measures confidence in a detected region, not the probability that a person's identity is known. Photo sharpness is a Laplacian-variance heuristic. Neither is a certified recognition-accuracy measurement.

Why the 512 values matter

A trained neural network encodes visual texture in a 512-dimensional embedding. Cosine similarity compares the directions of two embeddings: cos(A,B) = (A · B) / (||A|| ||B||). The 16 bars shown here are grouped means, not all 512 raw values.

What would count as a trustworthy match?

We must test genuinely distinct captures of the same eyes and captures of different eyes, then choose a threshold using an independent evaluation set. Report false-accept and false-reject rates, sample count, lighting conditions and failure-to-enroll cases. Until then, comparison outcomes are experimental.

Privacy, reuse and limitations

Iris images are sensitive biometric information. Upload only photos you have permission to use. The current server handles uploaded images for analysis; do not assume third-party hosting is private without checking deployment retention and logging. Model training provenance and licenses must be checked before public release.

Method inspiration: rubber-sheet iris normalization (Daugman), iris segmentation (YOLO), embedding learning (ArcIris/ResNet). Consult the project repository for implementation and component licensing.

CHAPTER 05 — BEYOND THE IMAGE

Not magic.
Mathematics & learning.

Follow the three ideas that make the full HAWIYA pipeline work. These are the choices behind the code, not just labels on a flowchart.

01 / SEEING

YOLO doesn't know a name. It knows a shape.

Our segmentation model predicts two regions: the iris, a textured colored ring, and the pupil, its dark opening. We use these regions to measure two boundaries. When the image is too blurred, too distant, or poorly lit, the system may not find both regions—and the pipeline stops rather than inventing a result.

It producesPixel masks → fitted circles → geometric coordinates
02 / GEOMETRY

One standard shape from many photographs.

The pupil changes size, even in the same person. A fixed crop of the image would move across different iris details. Instead, the rubber-sheet method samples between the pupil boundary and the outer iris boundary at every angle, forming a rectangle of 64 rows by 512 columns.

It produces64 × 512 sampled texture, with two prepared views
03 / LEARNING

Five hundred and twelve numbers. One texture description.

ResNet100 in ArcIris is a trained neural network. It reads the normalized pattern and returns a feature vector with 512 values. To compare two vectors, we calculate cosine similarity, based on the angle between them. The score measures likeness; it is not a person's identity or a probability of being the same person.

It produces512-dimensional vectors → cosine similarity
THE COMPARISON, EXPLAINED
similarity(A, B) = Σ(Aᵢ × Bᵢ) / (‖A‖ × ‖B‖)

We compare numerical patterns, not eye colors. The more similar the directions of two feature vectors, the larger the cosine value. A matching decision requires independent testing and carefully chosen thresholds.
